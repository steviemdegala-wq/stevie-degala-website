If you are ready to buy a new home in Gilmer TX but your current home has not sold yet, you might feel stuck. That is where a bridge loan comes in. A bridge loan is a short term loan that uses the equity in your existing home to help you buy your next one. It literally bridges the gap between selling and buying. If this sounds like your situation, here is what you need to know.

## What Is a Bridge Loan and How Does It Work?

A bridge loan is a temporary loan, usually six to twelve months long. It lets you tap into the equity you already have in your home and use it as a down payment on your next purchase. Once your original home sells, you pay off the bridge loan.

This means you do not have to make your offer contingent on selling your current home first. In a competitive market, that can make a real difference. Sellers tend to prefer buyers who do not have a contingency, because it means fewer conditions on the deal and a smoother path to closing.

The loan amount is typically based on the equity you have in your current home. Your lender will look at how much your home is worth and how much you still owe on it. The difference is what you can borrow against.

## Bridge Loans in Gilmer TX: Who They Work Best For

Bridge loans are not for everyone, but they can be a great fit in the right situation.

You have found the right home in Gilmer or Upshur County and you do not want to lose it while waiting for your current home to sell. You have good equity built up in your existing home. You are confident your property will move within a few months. If those sound familiar, a bridge loan might be exactly what you need.

Keep in mind that you will carry two mortgages for a short overlap period. That can be manageable if you have solid income and your finances are in good shape. It is worth running the numbers with your mortgage broker before you commit.

## How Bridge Loans Compare to Other Options

You might be wondering how a bridge loan stacks up against other choices. Here is a quick look.

A home equity line of credit, often called a HELOC, is another way to borrow against your home equity. But a HELOC takes longer to set up and usually requires your home not to be listed for sale. If you are already in the middle of a move, timing can be tricky.

A contingent offer lets you buy a home only if yours sells first. That can work in some cases, but sellers in competitive areas sometimes pass on contingent offers. It can cost you the home you really want.

A bridge loan moves quickly and is designed specifically for this in between moment. That speed and flexibility is what makes it worth considering.

## Rates, Fees, and What to Expect

Bridge loans typically carry higher interest rates than a standard mortgage. That is because they are short term and carry a bit more risk for the lender. You are usually looking at rates that are a point or two above a traditional 30 year mortgage rate.

The good news is that the loan is meant to be short lived. You are not locked in for decades. Once your home sells, you pay it off. Many borrowers find that the cost of the bridge loan is worth the peace of mind it provides.

Fees can also be higher than a conventional mortgage. Ask your lender up front about origination fees, appraisal costs, and closing costs so you go in with a clear picture of what you will spend.

## Getting Approved for a Bridge Loan in Gilmer TX

Lenders look at a few key things when approving a bridge loan. Your credit score matters. Your income and debt to income ratio matter. And the equity in your current home is a major factor.

In Upshur County and around the Gilmer area, rural properties sometimes take longer to sell than homes in larger cities. Keep that timeline in mind when thinking through whether a bridge loan is a good fit for you. If your property is a larger rural acreage or has unique features like a shop, barn, or well and septic, talk to your lender about realistic expectations for how long it might take to sell.

Your lender will want to see that you can comfortably handle both mortgage payments during the overlap period, even if that overlap is only a few months. Having a clear plan and open communication with your lender makes the whole process much smoother.

## FAQ: Bridge Loans in Gilmer TX

**How long does a bridge loan last?**
Most bridge loans are set up for six to twelve months. That gives you enough time to sell your current home and pay off the loan. Some lenders offer up to 24 months if your situation calls for it.

**Can I use a bridge loan on a rural property in Upshur County?**
Yes. Bridge loans can be used on rural properties. However, rural homes can take longer to sell. Talk with your lender about the realistic timeline for your area and make sure you have a solid plan in place.

**What happens if my home does not sell before the bridge loan is due?**
Your lender will work with you on options. This might include extending the loan term or transitioning into a different loan product. The key is to communicate early and not wait until the last minute.

**Do I need a certain credit score for a bridge loan?**
Most lenders look for a credit score of at least 650 to 680. The stronger your credit, the better your rate and terms will be. Talk to your broker early so you know exactly where you stand.

**Is a bridge loan the same as a hard money loan?**
No. A hard money loan is typically used by real estate investors for short term property flips. A bridge loan is designed for homeowners who need to buy a new home before they sell their current one. They have similar structures but serve very different purposes.
