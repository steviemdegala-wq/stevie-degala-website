## What Is a Land Loan?

A land loan is a type of financing used to purchase a piece of property that does not yet have a home on it. That might be a wooded parcel out in Gregg County, a cleared residential lot near town, or rural acreage you plan to build on in a few years.

Because lenders cannot use a finished home as collateral the way they can with a standard mortgage, land loans tend to come with a few extra requirements. Lenders see undeveloped land as higher risk, so they typically want a larger down payment and a strong credit profile. But there are good options out there, and a local broker can help you navigate them.

## Types of Land in Gregg County and How Lenders View Each One

Not all land is treated the same when it comes to financing.

Raw land is completely undeveloped. No utilities, no road access, no improvements at all. This type is the hardest to finance because lenders have fewer ways to assess its value and less confidence about what will happen with it. Down payments often run 25 to 35 percent for raw land, and interest rates tend to be higher.

Unimproved land is a step up from raw. It might have some infrastructure nearby, like a road or a utility line that has not been connected yet. Financing is a bit easier but still requires a meaningful down payment.

Improved lots have access to roads, water, sewer, and electricity. This is the easiest type to finance. Some lenders treat improved residential lots almost like a standard home purchase, with down payments starting around 10 to 20 percent.

If you are shopping for land near Longview, whether along the outer edges of the city, near Hallsville, or further out toward rural Gregg County, knowing what type of land you are looking at helps you understand what to expect from lenders before you make an offer.

## What Lenders Look for with Land Loans Longview TX

When you apply for a land loan in the Longview area, lenders review several things.

Your credit score. Most lenders want to see 680 or higher for land financing, though some programs have room to work with a slightly lower score depending on other factors.

Your down payment. Plan for 20 to 30 percent in most situations. Raw land may require more. Improved residential lots may allow less.

Your debt and income. Lenders want to see that your monthly income comfortably covers the land loan payment on top of your other bills.

Your plans for the land. Are you planning to build a home? Invest in the property? Start a small farm? Lenders ask because the purpose affects their assessment of the risk.

The land itself. Access to utilities, road access, flood zone status, and the overall condition of the property all factor into how a lender values the collateral.

## Land Loans vs. Construction Loans: What Is the Difference?

These two loan types often go hand in hand, but they are separate products.

A land loan covers the purchase of the property. A construction loan covers the cost of building a home on it later. You might use a land loan now to secure the parcel while you plan and save, then apply for a construction loan when you are ready to break ground.

Some lenders also offer combination products that bundle both stages into one loan. These can simplify the process if you plan to start building within a year or two of purchasing the land. A local mortgage broker can walk you through which approach makes more sense for your timeline and budget.

## Tips Before You Apply for a Land Loan in East Texas

A little preparation goes a long way.

Have a clear plan. Lenders feel more confident when you can describe what you intend to do with the land and offer a general timeline. Even a rough plan helps.

Get a survey done. Knowing the exact boundaries, total acreage, and any easements on the property protects you and gives your lender confidence.

Check utilities and road access before you make an offer. If power and water are already nearby, that is a meaningful advantage. Your broker can help you know what to ask.

Talk to a local broker early. A broker who knows the Longview and Gregg County market understands what lenders in East Texas look for and can match you with the right product for your situation from the start.

## FAQ: Land Loans in Longview TX

**Can I get a land loan with less than 20 percent down?**

It depends on the type of land and the lender. Improved lots in a subdivision sometimes qualify for loans with 10 to 15 percent down if your credit is strong. Raw land almost always requires more. Your broker can help you find the options that fit your situation.

**Are interest rates higher on land loans than on home loans?**

Yes, usually. Lenders charge a higher rate for land because it carries more risk than a property with a finished home already on it. The rate difference depends on the lender, the land type, and your credit profile.

**How long are land loan terms?**

Land loans often have shorter terms than a standard 30 year mortgage. Terms of 5, 10, or 15 years are common. That means higher monthly payments than you might expect from the loan balance alone, so factor that into your budget.

**Can I buy land and build a home with the same loan?**

Some lenders offer combination lot and construction loans that roll the land purchase and construction costs into one product. This simplifies the process but typically requires more planning upfront. Ask your broker whether a combined loan makes sense for your timeline.

**What should I check before making an offer on East Texas land?**

A few things worth confirming before you sign: utility availability, road access, flood zone status, and whether the property has any easements or deed restrictions. A survey and a title search are both worth the investment. Your broker can help you know what questions to ask before committing.
