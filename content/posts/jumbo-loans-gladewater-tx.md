## What Is a Jumbo Loan?

Most home loans get purchased by two companies called Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac after closing. These companies set a maximum on how large a loan they will accept. That maximum is called the conforming loan limit. Any loan under that number is a conforming loan. Any loan over it is a jumbo loan.

The conforming loan limit changes each year and can vary by county. For 2026, the baseline limit in most areas of Texas is $766,550. If your home loan is larger than that, you are in jumbo territory.

## How Are Jumbo Loans Different From Regular Mortgages?

The core difference is risk. With a standard loan, your lender can sell it to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac after closing, which frees up cash to make new loans. With a jumbo loan, the lender holds onto it. That means they take on more risk. To manage that risk, lenders set stricter guidelines than what you see with a conventional loan.

You will generally need a credit score of at least 700 to qualify. Most lenders also want to see a down payment of 10 to 20 percent and enough savings in reserve to cover several months of mortgage payments. Your debt to income ratio, which is the share of your monthly income that goes toward debt payments, should usually stay below 43 percent.

That said, lenders vary quite a bit on their exact requirements. One lender may want 20 percent down while another will approve you with 10. Working with a broker who can compare options across multiple lenders means you are not limited to just one set of rules.

## What to Expect When You Apply

The jumbo loan process is straightforward once you know what to gather. You will need recent pay stubs or proof of income, two years of tax returns, two to three months of bank statements, and information about any other assets you have such as retirement accounts or investments.

The lender will order an appraisal on the property. Some jumbo lenders require two independent appraisals on higher value properties. This adds a small cost and a few extra days to the process, but it protects you and the lender both.

Most jumbo loans close in 30 to 45 days. If you have your documents ready and the title work comes back clean, the timeline can move on the faster end of that range.

Not necessarily. Some lenders allow as little as 10 percent down on a jumbo loan. You may see a slightly higher rate at a lower down payment, but it is absolutely an option for buyers who prefer to keep more cash available after closing.

**Can I get a jumbo loan for a rural property or land with a home?**
Yes, and this comes up often in East Texas. Lenders will appraise the property and the land together. Properties with significant acreage are common in the Gladewater area, and jumbo loans handle them regularly.

**What credit score do I need?**
