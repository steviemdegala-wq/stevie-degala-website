Fort Collins is where most of Northern Colorado's healthcare hiring happens. UCHealth Poudre Valley Hospital, Banner Health North Colorado Medical Center in adjacent Greeley, and UCHealth Medical Center of the Rockies in nearby Loveland all draw physicians, residents, and advanced practice providers to this corridor every year. If you are a medical professional buying in Fort Collins, the financing product built specifically for your profession is worth understanding before you look at anything else.

A [physician loan](/loans/doctor-loan) is a privately held mortgage product from select lenders designed around the financial profile of doctors, dentists, and other licensed healthcare professionals. The defining features — zero down payment, no PMI, and favorable treatment of student loan debt — are all consequential in Fort Collins's market.

Fort Collins is not a starter home market. Single-family homes in desirable neighborhoods — Old Town, Midtown, Harmony Corridor, Prospect — generally run $550,000 to $950,000+. The southwest side near Horsetooth tends to attract professionals who want proximity to both UCHealth facilities and the mountains.

Add closing costs of $12,000–$18,000 on top of a conventional down payment, and a physician starting their attending year at UCHealth is looking at $50,000–$100,000 in liquid assets required before getting keys. A physician loan removes the down payment component entirely — those funds stay available for student loan paydown, retirement contributions, or an emergency reserve during the transition from training.

No PMI compounds the benefit. On a $750,000 loan, PMI at a conventional rate would add $300–$500 per month to the payment. A physician loan carries neither the down payment requirement nor that monthly surcharge.

## How Physician Loans Handle Student Debt

This is where physician loans do the most important work for Fort Collins buyers.

Conventional underwriting uses what is called the 1% rule for student loans in deferment: if your balance is $250,000 and payments are paused, the lender assumes a monthly payment of $2,500 regardless of what you will actually pay. For income-driven repayment borrowers, the gap between the assumed payment and the actual payment can be enormous.

A physician with $250,000 in student loans on an IBR plan paying $400/month:

- **Conventional underwriting**: $2,500/month assumed → dramatically reduces qualifying income
- **Physician loan underwriting**: $400/month actual payment or $0 if fully deferred

On a $200,000 income, that $2,100 monthly swing translates to approximately $250,000+ in additional purchase price qualification. In Fort Collins, that difference is between qualifying for the neighborhood you want and being pushed to lower-cost markets.

Most physician loan programs treat student debt in one of three ways, depending on the lender: use the actual IBR/PAYE payment, exclude deferred balances entirely, or apply a reduced percentage. ## Residency and Fellowship Buyers at UCHealth

Residents and fellows at UCHealth programs have an additional advantage worth knowing.

Most physician loan programs allow an **employment contract** to substitute for income history. If you have signed an attending contract — whether with UCHealth, Banner, or a private practice in the Fort Collins area — and are still completing training elsewhere, you can often use that contract to qualify now.

This means: you identify the home in Fort Collins, go under contract, and close before your first attending paycheck. You do not need to rent for a year building a paper income trail while the market moves.

A few things to have ready if you are in this situation:
- Signed employment contract with start date and compensation
- Proof of licensing or diploma (depending on lender)
- Letter from your training program confirming anticipated completion

The timeline from offer to close is typically 30–45 days. Planning two to four months ahead of your start date is ideal so you are not rushing the process at the end of training.

## Who Qualifies for a Physician Loan in Fort Collins

Qualifying professions vary by lender, but the most common include:

**Doctoral-level (MD, DO, DDS, DMD, DVM)**: All major physician loan programs
**Advanced degrees with prescriptive authority (PharmD, CRNA)**: Most programs
**Advanced practice (NP, PA, CNM)**: Included in many programs

Some programs extend to physical therapists (DPT), optometrists (OD), and podiatrists (DPM). The easiest way to confirm is to review your specific credential together — I have mapped out which lenders accept which designations.

## Fort Collins Neighborhoods Worth Knowing for Physician Buyers

**Old Town area** ($700,000–$1.2M+): Walkability, character, proximity to UCHealth main campus. Popular with physicians who want urban amenity without commuting distance tradeoffs.

**Harmony Corridor / Fossil Creek** ($550,000–$800,000): Close to I-25, shorter commute east toward Greeley or south toward Loveland. More new construction at accessible price points.

**Midtown** ($500,000–$750,000): Closer to Colorado State University and central Fort Collins employers. Practical for dual-career households.

**Southwest Fort Collins / Horsetooth** ($650,000–$950,000): Mountain views, proximity to outdoor recreation, and a short drive to both UCHealth facilities. Attracts physicians who want quality of life beyond the commute.

For buyers whose hospital is in Greeley or Loveland, the communities just south of Fort Collins — Timnath, Windsor, Severance — often offer stronger value per dollar with easy I-25 access. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the maximum loan amount for a physician loan in Fort Collins?**
Most physician loan programs go up to $1.5 million or higher with zero down, and some offer jumbo options above that. For Fort Collins's price range, the standard program limits are sufficient for the majority of purchases.

**Do physician loans have higher interest rates than conventional loans?**
Rates are competitive and vary by lender, loan size, and your financial profile. Because physician loans carry no PMI and often no down payment, the effective cost of borrowing — rate plus PMI — is frequently comparable to or better than a conventional loan with a 5–10% down payment. I compare across lenders to find the best rate for your specific situation.

**Can I use a physician loan to buy in Fort Collins while still completing residency?**
Yes, if you have a signed employment contract for a position in the area. Many of my physician loan clients close before their first attending paycheck. Contact timing and documentation matter — the earlier you start, the smoother the process.

**Does it matter which hospital I work for?**
No. The program is based on your professional license, not your employer. UCHealth, Banner Health, private practice, locum tenens — the loan program works the same way regardless of employer type.

**Can physicians who are also real estate investors use this product?**
Yes. A physician loan is for your primary residence. Many physician investors use a physician loan on their home and [investor lines of credit](/loans/investor-line-of-credit) or DSCR loans for their investment portfolio separately. It is a clean division of purpose.
