## What Makes Manufactured Home Loans Different?

A manufactured home is built in a factory according to federal HUD building standards. That is different from a modular home, which is built in sections and assembled on site. Lenders treat manufactured homes a little differently, and not every standard mortgage applies. But the good news is that if your home is permanently attached to a foundation on land you own, most major loan programs are available to you.

The two main categories are real property loans and chattel loans. A real property loan treats the home much like a traditionally built house. A chattel loan is for homes on leased land or titled as personal property. Real property loans give you better rates and more program options. It is worth understanding which category your home falls into before you start applying.

## Manufactured Home Loans in Windsor CO: Your Program Options

Here are the loan programs that work for manufactured homes in Windsor and Weld County.

**FHA Loans.** The Federal Housing Administration backs loans for manufactured homes on permanent foundations. You can qualify with as little as 3.5 percent down and a credit score of 580 or higher. This is one of the most accessible programs available, especially if you are a first-time buyer or working with a smaller down payment.

**VA Loans.** The Front Range has a strong military community, and if you are a veteran or active duty service member, VA loans cover manufactured homes too. The home must be on a permanent foundation and classified as real property. No down payment is required for qualified borrowers, which makes this one of the best deals you will find anywhere.

**USDA Loans.** Parts of Weld County outside Windsor's city limits qualify for USDA rural housing programs. USDA offers zero down payment for buyers who meet income guidelines. Manufactured homes on permanent foundations can qualify, making this a great option if you are buying on a rural parcel outside of town.

**Conventional Loans.** Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac both offer programs for manufactured homes on permanent foundations. These usually require stronger credit and a larger down payment, but they come with competitive rates and you can often drop mortgage insurance once you build enough equity.

## What Lenders Look at in Windsor and Weld County

Before a lender approves a manufactured home loan, they check a few key things. First, is the home classified as real property? That means it sits on a permanent, HUD compliant foundation. The axles, wheels, and tow hitch must be removed. The home title should be converted from a vehicle title to a real estate deed. Many manufactured homes in Weld County already meet these requirements, especially established properties on owned land.

Your credit score and income history matter just like they do with any mortgage. FHA and VA are more forgiving for lower scores. Conventional loans reward stronger credit with better rates and lower fees. Lenders also want to see steady employment, ideally two years or more in the same field.

The age and condition of the home is another factor. Newer homes have an easier path to financing. Some programs limit loans on homes built before a certain year. If you are looking at an older home, ask about this early so there are no surprises later in the process.

## FAQ: Manufactured Home Loans in Windsor CO

**Do I need to own the land to get a real estate loan?**
Yes. For FHA, VA, USDA, and conventional programs, the home needs to be on land you own or are buying at the same time. If the home is in a community with a land lease, chattel financing is usually the path forward.

**What credit score do I need?**
FHA loans accept scores as low as 580 with 3.5 percent down. VA and USDA have flexible guidelines depending on the lender. Conventional loans generally want 620 or higher. Better scores mean better rates, so it is worth taking a few months to build your score if you are close to a threshold.

**Can I finance a manufactured home the same way I would a regular house?**
Yes, if the home is on a permanent foundation and titled as real property, you can often finance it very similarly to a traditionally built home. Many buyers in Windsor and Weld County go this route with great results.

**How long does the loan process take?**
Most loans close in 30 to 45 days. USDA loans can take a bit longer because of a secondary review step from the government. Getting your documents together early is the single best thing you can do to keep things on track.
