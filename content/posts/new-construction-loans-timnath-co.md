## What Is a New Construction Loan?

A new construction loan is a short term loan that covers the cost of building your home from the ground up. Instead of receiving one big lump sum, the money is released in stages as your home gets built. This is called a draw schedule. Your builder submits requests as each phase is completed, and the lender releases funds to pay for that phase.

Once your home is finished, you have two paths. You can refinance into a permanent mortgage, which means two closings. Or you can use a one time close loan, which converts into a regular mortgage automatically when the build is done. The one time close option saves you from paying closing costs twice, which is a real benefit worth asking about.

## Loan Types to Consider for Your New Build

You have a few solid options when it comes to financing a new construction home.

**One Time Close Loans.** You apply once, close once, and your loan converts to a permanent mortgage when the home is finished. Fewer fees and less paperwork.

**Two Time Close Loans.** You take out a construction loan first and then refinance into a mortgage once the home is built. This can give you more flexibility on the back end, but you do pay closing costs twice.

**FHA Construction Loans.** If you want a lower down payment, an FHA construction loan may let you put down as little as 3.5 percent. Great for buyers who have good income but not a huge pile of savings.

**VA Construction Loans.** If you served in the military, a VA construction loan may allow you to build a brand new home with no money down. This is one of the best benefits available to veterans, and it is underused.

**Conventional Construction Loans.** If you have strong credit and a solid down payment, a conventional construction loan often gives you the most flexibility in loan terms and builder requirements.

## What Lenders Want to See

Construction loans tend to have a little more scrutiny than a regular purchase loan. Here is what most lenders look at.

**Credit.** A score of 620 or higher is a common starting point, though some programs can work with a bit less.

**Down Payment.** Expect 10 to 20 percent for most conventional construction loans. FHA and VA programs can bring that number down a lot.

**An Approved Builder.** Your builder usually needs to be licensed and meet the lender's requirements. Most established builders in the Timnath area are already set up to work with major lenders.

**Construction Plans and Budget.** Your lender will want a signed contract, a project timeline, and a detailed budget. The more organized your builder is, the smoother this step goes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a new construction loan last?**
Most run for 6 to 12 months. If your builder finishes on time, that is plenty of runway. Some lenders offer extensions if delays come up.

**Do I make payments while my home is being built?**
Yes, but only on the amount that has been drawn so far. As more funds get released, your interest only payment goes up a little. Once you convert to a permanent mortgage, your full payment kicks in.

**Can I pick my own finishes and upgrades?**
Absolutely. That is one of the best parts of building new. Just make sure any upgrades are included in your signed contract and budget before closing. Surprises added later can create headaches.

**What if the build goes over budget?**
It happens. Some construction loans include a small contingency reserve for exactly this reason. Either way, it is smart to keep some extra savings set aside in case costs run a little higher than expected.

**Can I lock in my mortgage rate now?**
It depends on the lender and loan type. Some one time close programs let you lock your permanent rate at the time of closing. Ask your broker about rate lock options before you choose a loan.
