Greeley is not the same housing market it was five years ago. Weld County home prices have moved steadily upward, and medical professionals relocating to work at Banner Health or the UCHealth network are arriving into a market where the cost of waiting — while saving for a conventional down payment — is real and measurable.

If you are a physician, nurse practitioner, resident, or other medical professional buying in Greeley, Windsor, or surrounding Weld County communities, here is what you should know about using a [physician loan](/loans/doctor-loan) in this market.

## Why Greeley Is Worth Understanding Separately

Greeley sits in Weld County, which has its own home price dynamics compared to Larimer County. While Fort Collins typically commands higher prices, Greeley's market has compressed in recent years — and the homes in the $400,000 to $650,000 range that are common in the area still trigger meaningful PMI costs on a conventional loan with less than 20% down.

For a medical professional buying a $525,000 home in Greeley with 5% down on a conventional loan, PMI can run $175 to $250 per month — on top of a principle and interest payment that already reflects current rates. A physician loan removes that cost entirely.

## Banner Health and UCHealth: Two Different Employer Profiles

Greeley's two dominant healthcare employers attract different borrower profiles, but both tend to involve the same structural financing challenge: significant student loan balances alongside solid income.

**Banner Health (Banner Health North Colorado Medical Center)** is one of the largest employers in the city. Physicians, advanced practice providers, and clinical staff in hospital-based roles often carry student debt from longer training tracks — medical school, residency, fellowship — that would substantially increase their DTI under conventional loan guidelines.

**UCHealth (in Greeley's broader network)** draws residents and early-career attendings who may be just establishing their financial footprint. A physician loan's ability to accept a signed employment contract in place of pay stubs is particularly relevant for anyone who has recently joined and has not yet filed a full year of W-2s.

## How Student Loans Work Against You in Greeley's Market

Conventional loan guidelines require lenders to count your student loan payment in your debt-to-income ratio. If you are on an income-driven repayment plan, some programs will use 1% of your total loan balance per month as the assumed payment — even if your actual payment is lower.

For a physician with $200,000 in student loans, that is an extra $2,000 per month added to your monthly debt load before the new mortgage is even factored in. That calculation can reduce what you qualify for by $200,000 to $300,000 on the purchase price side.

Physician loan programs treat student debt differently. Many exclude loans that are in deferment. Others use your actual IBR payment. The result is that your qualifying income stretches meaningfully further — often enough to reach the home price you are actually targeting in Weld County.

## Residents and New Hires: Buying Before the First Paycheck

If you have accepted a position at Banner Health or are completing a residency in the Greeley area and planning to buy before your start date, a physician loan may be the only product that allows that. Most physician loan programs will accept a fully executed employment contract as documentation of your income — conventional loans will not.

This matters in Greeley's market because the inventory in the $450,000 to $600,000 range that suits early-career professionals moves quickly. Being able to go under contract before you start — rather than waiting six months to establish a paper trail — can mean the difference between the neighborhood you want and settling.

## The Down Payment Question

Physician loans in Colorado generally allow:

- 0% down up to certain loan amounts
- 5% down for higher loan amounts (varies by program)
- No PMI regardless of down payment percentage

For a $525,000 home in Greeley, a zero-down physician loan versus a 5% conventional loan means keeping $26,250 in your pocket — capital that stays liquid rather than locked into equity you cannot access without refinancing or selling.

For a physician who is also paying down student loans or building an emergency fund after a long training period, that difference is material.

## When a Conventional Loan Makes More Sense

Physician loans are not the right answer in every situation. If you have a full 20% down payment available and your student debt is minimal or already paid down, a conventional loan at that tier often comes with a lower rate and no funding considerations unique to physician products.

The right answer is specific to your numbers — loan size, credit, student debt balance, how long you plan to stay in the home. I run both scenarios side by side with real rate quotes before you commit to anything.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do physician loans work in Greeley the same way they do in Fort Collins?**
Yes. Physician loan programs are not city-specific. If you qualify for a physician loan, you can use it to buy in Greeley, Windsor, Evans, Eaton, or any other Weld County community. The property just needs to be a primary residence.

**I work at Banner Health. Does my employer matter for qualification?**
Your employer does not need to be a specific institution. What matters is your professional credential. If you hold a qualifying degree or license and your income supports the loan, the employer is largely irrelevant to your physician loan eligibility.

**Can I use a physician loan for a new construction home in Windsor or Severance?**
Yes. Physician loans can be used for new construction, though the process has a few additional steps compared to an existing home purchase — including a spec or pre-sale agreement and a VA-style progress inspection for some programs. I handle this regularly in the Windsor and Severance communities.

**What if I have already filed taxes as an independent contractor?**
Self-employed medical professionals — including those who contract through staffing agencies or work locum tenens — have specific documentation requirements. I work with programs that handle W-2, 1099, and mixed income structures. It is worth a conversation before you assume you do not qualify.
