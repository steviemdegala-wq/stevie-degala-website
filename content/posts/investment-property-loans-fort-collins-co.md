## Types of Investment Property Loans in Fort Collins CO

Not all investment property loans are the same. Here are the main options worth knowing about.

**Conventional Investment Property Loans**

A conventional loan is the most common path for buying a rental property. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac allow conventional financing on investment properties, though the guidelines are stricter than for a primary home. You will typically need at least 15 to 25 percent down, a solid credit score, and enough reserves to cover several months of payments. The interest rate will be a bit higher than what you would see on a home you live in yourself. That is normal and expected.

**DSCR Loans**

DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. This is a loan designed specifically for rental properties, and it qualifies you based on the property's income rather than your personal income. If the rent covers the mortgage payment, you can often qualify even if your personal tax returns show limited income. This type of loan is popular with investors who work for themselves and people who hold multiple properties. Fort Collins rental income tends to support strong DSCR ratios, which makes this loan type a natural fit here.

**Hard Money and Bridge Loans**

If you are buying a distressed property to fix up and either hold or sell, a hard money or bridge loan can get you into the deal quickly. These are loans with shorter terms and higher rates, but they close fast and care more about the property's value than your personal income. I work with lenders who offer these options for the right situations.

## What to Expect on Down Payment and Rates

Investment property financing requires more skin in the game than a primary home. Most conventional programs require 15 percent down for a single unit investment property and 25 percent for a two to four unit building. DSCR loans often start at 20 to 25 percent down as well.

Interest rates on investment properties run higher than primary residence rates. How much higher depends on your credit score, the loan amount, and the specific program. The good news is that Fort Collins rents are strong enough that a property chosen well can still cash flow meaningfully even at today's rates.

## How Lenders Look at Your Finances

When you apply for a conventional investment property loan, lenders review your full financial picture. They look at your income, existing debt, credit history, and how many investment properties you already own. Fannie Mae allows up to 10 financed properties for experienced investors, but lenders may have their own caps below that.

With a DSCR loan, the focus shifts to the rental income the property generates. Lenders calculate the DSCR by dividing the monthly rental income by the monthly mortgage payment, taxes, insurance, and any HOA fees. A ratio of 1.0 means the property breaks even. Most lenders want to see at least 1.0 to 1.25 depending on the program.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do I need to put down on an investment property in Fort Collins?**

Most conventional programs require at least 15 percent down for a single unit and 25 percent for a two to four unit property. DSCR loans generally start at 20 to 25 percent down. The exact amount depends on the loan type and your overall profile.

**Can I use rental income from the new property to help me qualify?**

It depends on the loan type. With a DSCR loan, yes, the property's projected rental income is the main qualifying factor. With a conventional loan, lenders may count a portion of the rental income after applying a vacancy factor, and they typically want a signed lease or rental history to do so.

**What credit score do I need for an investment property loan in Fort Collins?**

Conventional investment loans typically require a minimum score around 620 to 640, though better rates come with scores in the 700s. DSCR loans have their own score requirements, often starting around 640 to 660. I can tell you exactly what you need once I understand your full situation.

**Is it harder to get approved for an investment property loan than a primary home loan?**

Yes, the standards are stricter. Lenders see investment properties as higher risk because borrowers are more likely to walk away from a rental than from the home they live in. That means higher down payments, higher rates, and more scrutiny on your reserves and overall financial picture.

**Can I convert my primary home to a rental and use those rents to buy again?**

Yes, and this is a common strategy. Once you move out, the property becomes an investment property. Lenders typically allow you to count 75 percent of the rental income toward your qualifying income once you have a signed lease in place. This can make it easier to qualify for your next purchase.
