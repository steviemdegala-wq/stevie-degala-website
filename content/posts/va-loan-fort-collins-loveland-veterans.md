Larimer County's housing market has been one of the more competitive in Colorado's Front Range for years. Fort Collins consistently draws buyers from across the state and nationally — for its proximity to outdoor recreation, its university, and its employer base. Loveland has followed closely, with appreciation that has made the gap between renting and owning feel larger with every passing year.

For veterans and active duty military buying in either city, the [VA loan](/loans/va) is not just a benefit — it is often the most financially rational path to ownership. Here is how the program actually works in this market, and what to know before you start looking.

## The Larimer County Market in Real Terms

Home prices in Fort Collins and Loveland have pushed median sale prices well above the statewide average. A primary residence in the $475,000 to $700,000 range is common for buyers targeting single-family homes in established neighborhoods.

At those price points, the cost difference between a VA loan and a conventional loan becomes concrete:

- A veteran buying a $600,000 home in Fort Collins with a VA loan puts **zero dollars down** and pays **no PMI**
- The same buyer on a conventional loan with 5% down ($30,000) would also carry PMI — likely $200 to $325 per month — until reaching 20% equity
- That PMI alone costs $2,400 to $3,900 per year, every year, until the loan balance drops below 80% of the original purchase price

The VA funding fee — typically 2.15% for a first-time VA borrower with no down payment — can be rolled into the loan balance. For a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or greater, the fee is waived entirely. In most comparison scenarios, the VA loan wins on total cost of ownership.

## Fort Collins: What Buyers Should Expect

Fort Collins is an active buyer's market with limited days on market for well-priced homes. VA loans have historically carried a stigma among some listing agents and sellers who perceived them as slower or more likely to have appraisal issues. That perception has shifted considerably, but it is worth addressing directly.

A VA appraisal is not the same as a standard appraisal. It also checks for Minimum Property Requirements — basic habitability and safety standards. For homes in good condition, this is rarely an obstacle. For older homes or properties with deferred maintenance (peeling paint on pre-1978 construction, roof issues, water damage), the MPR check can require repairs before closing.

I flag potential MPR issues early in the process for exactly this reason. If we identify a property that is likely to have conditions, we address it in the offer or in negotiation before you are under contract.

## Loveland: A Value Play That Veterans Are Using Well

Loveland typically prices 10–15% below comparable Fort Collins properties, which makes VA loan purchasing power particularly meaningful here. Veterans who are flexible on city — willing to look in Loveland rather than anchoring on Fort Collins — often find they can access a meaningfully better property for the same payment.

Loveland also has a growing healthcare employer base (Banner McKee Medical Center, UCHealth), so medical professionals who also carry VA eligibility have an unusually strong set of financing options in this market.

## VA Purchase in Timnath, Windsor, and Severance

The new construction communities along the I-25 corridor — Timnath, Windsor, Severance — are popular with veterans looking for modern homes in growing areas. VA loans work for new construction, though with some additional steps:

- You will need a VA-approved appraisal, which for new construction may be ordered at a specific stage of the build
- Most builders require a one-year warranty on new construction, which VA guidelines also mandate
- The builder and the specific community need to meet VA property standards

I have worked through new construction VA transactions in these communities. The process is manageable — it just requires coordination with the builder's team early.

Before anything else, confirm you have a Certificate of Eligibility (COE). This is the document that tells your lender you have earned VA loan benefits.

I pull COEs directly through VA systems. In most cases, it takes minutes. If your records require additional documentation — particularly for Guard or Reserve members, or for veterans with less common service classifications — I will tell you exactly what to gather. You do not need to navigate the VA's systems yourself.

## Who Is Eligible in Northern Colorado

- **Veterans** discharged under other than dishonorable conditions after meeting service minimums
- **Active duty service members** with 90+ continuous days during wartime or 181+ days during peacetime
- **National Guard and Reserve members** with 6+ years of service, or called to active duty under Title 10 or Title 32
- **Surviving spouses** of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability

If you are not certain whether you qualify, a five-minute conversation resolves it. Eligibility is the first thing I confirm — before we talk about anything else.

## VA Programs Available to You in Larimer County

**VA Purchase Loan** — Buy a home in Fort Collins, Loveland, or anywhere in Larimer County with zero down, no PMI, and a government-backed interest rate.

**VA Cash-Out Refinance** — If you already own a home and have built equity, you can access up to 100% of your home's appraised value in many cases. This is also the mechanism to convert a non-VA loan into a VA loan.

**VA IRRRL (Streamline Refinance)** — If you already have a VA loan and rates have dropped, the IRRRL lets you lower your rate with minimal documentation and no appraisal in most cases. The fastest refinance option available.

## Disability Ratings and the Funding Fee

If you have a service-connected disability rating of 10% or greater, the VA funding fee is waived entirely. This is one of the most significant and most often missed aspects of the program.

On a $600,000 purchase in Fort Collins with no down payment, the 2.15% funding fee is $12,900. For a veteran with a qualifying disability rating, that cost goes to zero. I confirm disability status early in the process so we know the full picture before you make any commitments.

Colorado also offers a property tax exemption for veterans with a disability rating of 50% or higher — an additional annual savings that compounds over the life of ownership.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it harder to get an offer accepted with a VA loan in Fort Collins?**
It used to be more of an issue. In today's market, most experienced agents and sellers are accustomed to VA offers. Having a strong preapproval letter, clear communication about the timeline, and working with a broker who can explain VA loan mechanics to a listing agent goes a long way. I have seen VA offers accepted in competitive situations regularly.

**Can I use my VA benefit if I already used it once before?**
Yes. VA loan benefits can be reused as long as you have remaining entitlement — which is restored when you sell a previous home and pay off the prior VA loan. Even if you have used your benefit before, it is worth confirming your current entitlement position before assuming you cannot use it again.

**What is the maximum loan amount for a VA loan in Fort Collins?**
For veterans with full entitlement, there is no official loan limit. The VA does not cap how much you can borrow, as long as your income supports the payment and the lender approves the file. This changed with the Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2020, which eliminated loan limits for full-entitlement borrowers.

**How quickly can I close on a VA loan in Loveland?**
VA loans do not inherently take longer than conventional loans. A well-prepared file with a clean COE, complete documentation, and no MPR issues can close in 30 days or fewer. The variable is usually documentation gathering and property condition, not the VA process itself.
