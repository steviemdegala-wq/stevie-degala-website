## What Is a Physician Loan?

A physician loan is a home loan that lenders created specifically for medical professionals. Most lenders offer it to doctors, dentists, veterinarians, and sometimes other licensed healthcare providers. The big differences are things that help people who are just starting their careers after years of school.

Here is what makes a physician loan stand out:

No private mortgage insurance. With a standard loan, if you put less than 20% down, you usually pay PMI every month. That can add hundreds of dollars to your payment. Physician loans skip that requirement entirely.

Low or no down payment. Many physician loans let you buy with 0% to 10% down, depending on the loan amount and the lender.

Flexible student debt guidelines. Medical school is expensive. Most doctors carry large student loan balances. A physician loan can count your student debt differently than a conventional loan, which may let you qualify for a higher home price.

## How Student Debt Affects Your Qualification

This is one of the most common concerns doctors have when they apply for a mortgage. You finished school, you have a good salary, but you also have a large student loan balance. On a standard mortgage, lenders count that full payment against your debt to income ratio, which is the percentage of your monthly income that goes toward debt payments.

With a physician loan, lenders often handle this differently. Some lenders exclude deferred student loans completely. Others use a lower percentage of your balance to calculate the payment. This can make a big difference in how much home you qualify for.

Say your student loans are currently deferred because you are in residency. A conventional lender might use 1% of your total balance as a monthly payment in their calculation. A physician loan lender may use zero. That difference alone can change your approval.

## Who Qualifies for a Physician Loan?

Lenders have different rules, so your specific situation matters. But in general, here is what most lenders look for:

You need to be a licensed medical professional. Common qualifying designations include MD, DO, DDS, DMD, DVM, and sometimes NP, PA, PharmD, and others depending on the lender.

You typically need to have a job offer or a signed employment contract if you have not started working yet. Lenders want to see that your income is coming.

Credit score requirements vary, but most lenders want at least a 700. Some will go lower depending on other factors.

The home usually needs to be your primary residence. Physician loans are for the home you live in, not investment properties or vacation homes.

Loan amounts vary too. Many physician loan programs go up to 1 million dollars or more without requiring a large down payment.
