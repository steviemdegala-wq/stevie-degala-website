## What Makes a Jumbo Loan Different From a Regular Mortgage?

Most home loans get sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac after closing. These two agencies set a limit on how large a loan they will accept. That limit is called the conforming loan limit. Anything under that number is a conforming loan. Anything over it is a jumbo loan.

The difference matters because lenders take on more risk with a jumbo loan. They cannot offload it to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac the way they do with smaller loans. So they set their own rules, which are usually a little stricter.

You will typically need a credit score of at least 700 to qualify. You will also need a down payment of 10 to 20 percent and documentation showing steady income and healthy savings. Some lenders require more. Others are more flexible. That is why working with a local broker who can compare options across multiple lenders makes a real difference.

## How Big Is Too Big for a Regular Loan?

The conforming loan limit changes from year to year and varies by location. In Gregg County, the limit follows the national baseline. For 2026, that baseline sits at $766,550. Any home loan in Longview above that amount is a jumbo loan.

If you are buying a home priced over $900,000 and putting 20 percent down, you would still be borrowing well above that baseline, which means a jumbo loan would be in play.

## What to Expect During the Jumbo Loan Process

The jumbo loan process looks a lot like any other mortgage. You apply, the lender reviews your credit and income, and they order an appraisal on the property. The main difference is that lenders take a closer look at your overall financial picture.

Expect to provide two years of tax returns, two to three months of bank statements, and documentation for any other assets you hold such as retirement accounts or investment portfolios. Lenders want to see that you have enough reserves to keep making payments even if your income temporarily dips.

Most jumbo loans close in 30 to 45 days. Some lenders in the East Texas market can move faster when everything is in order.

## FAQ: Jumbo Loans in Longview, TX

**Can I get a jumbo loan with less than 20 percent down?**
Yes. Some lenders allow as little as 10 percent down on a jumbo loan. You may see a slightly higher rate, but it is absolutely possible and many Longview buyers go this route.

**Are jumbo loans available for second homes or investment properties?**
Yes, though the requirements are usually a bit stricter for non-primary residences. If you are looking at a vacation home or a rental property in East Texas, ask your broker about your options.

**Do I need perfect credit to qualify?**
Not perfect, but strong. Most lenders want to see a score of at least 700. Some will work with scores in the high 680s, though your rate may reflect the added risk.

**What is debt-to-income ratio and why does it matter for a jumbo loan?**
Debt-to-income ratio is the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward debt payments, including your new mortgage. For jumbo loans, lenders generally want that number below 43 percent. Some will allow up to 45 or 50 percent if you have strong compensating factors like a large cash reserve.

**How is a jumbo loan appraisal different?**
Some jumbo lenders require two independent appraisals instead of one, especially for higher-priced properties. This adds a small cost and a bit of time but protects both you and the lender.
