## What Makes Investment Property Loans Different from Regular Home Loans

When you buy a home you plan to live in, lenders see that as lower risk. You have a personal stake in keeping up with payments. With an investment property, lenders look at the deal a little differently. They usually ask for a larger down payment, and the interest rate tends to be slightly higher. You will also need stronger credit and solid cash reserves.

None of that means investment property financing is out of reach. It just means you want to go in prepared. Knowing what lenders are looking for makes the whole thing easier.

## Common Loan Options for Investment Properties in Gilmer

There are a few different paths depending on what you are buying and what your goals are.

**Conventional investment loans** are one of the most common choices. With a conventional loan, you are typically looking at 15 to 25 percent down on an investment property. Your credit score matters here. Most lenders want to see 680 or better, and some prefer 700 or above. The good news is that these loans come with competitive rates and terms.

**DSCR loans**, which stands for debt service coverage ratio loans, are popular with real estate investors because they qualify based on the property's rental income rather than your personal income. If the rent the property brings in covers the mortgage payment, you have a strong case for approval. This is a solid option for self-employed investors or anyone whose personal income is hard to document the traditional way.

**Portfolio loans** come from lenders who hold the loan on their own books instead of selling it to the secondary market. These can be more flexible on things like the number of properties you already own or unusual property types.

Rental market conditions in Gilmer are different from what you would see in Longview or Tyler. A lender who understands the East Texas market can help you make sense of what the numbers need to look like for a deal to work.

## How Much Do You Need to Put Down

For most investment property loans, you should plan on at least 15 percent down for a single-unit property. Two to four unit properties usually require 25 percent. Some programs allow lower down payments, but they come with trade-offs like higher rates or mortgage insurance.

Your down payment amount affects your monthly cash flow, so it is worth thinking through carefully. A bigger down payment lowers your payment and improves your cash-on-cash return. A smaller down payment keeps more capital in your pocket for the next deal.

## Tips for Getting Approved on an Investment Property in Gilmer

A few things that help your approval process go smoothly:

Get your credit in good shape before you apply. Pay down revolving balances and avoid opening new accounts in the months leading up to your application.

Have cash reserves ready. Lenders want to see that you can cover several months of payments even if the property sits vacant for a stretch.

Have a clear picture of the rental market. If you are using a DSCR loan, the projected rent matters. An appraiser will look at comparable rents in the area, so having that research ready shows lenders you know the deal.

Work with a broker who knows investment property loans. The program that fits you best depends on your full financial picture and what kind of property you are buying.

## FAQ: Investment Property Loans in Gilmer, TX

**Can I use an FHA loan to buy an investment property?**
FHA loans are for primary residences only. You have to plan to live in the home to use an FHA loan. However, you can buy a duplex or small multifamily with FHA if you live in one unit.

**What credit score do I need for an investment property loan?**
Most conventional investment property loans want a credit score of at least 680. DSCR loans and some portfolio products can sometimes go a little lower, but better credit always gets you better terms.

**How many investment properties can I have?**
Conventional guidelines generally allow up to 10 financed properties, but individual lenders may have their own limits. DSCR and portfolio loans often have more flexibility on this.

**Do I need to show rental income on my taxes to qualify?**
With a standard conventional loan, lenders may look at your rental history. With a DSCR loan, the property's projected or current rent does most of the qualifying work, so documented rental income on your returns is less critical.

**What is the interest rate on an investment property loan?**
Investment property rates are typically 0.5 to 1 percent higher than a primary residence rate, sometimes more depending on the loan type and your profile. Rates change often, so always check current numbers with your lender.
