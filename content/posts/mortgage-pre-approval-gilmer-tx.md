## What Is a Mortgage Pre-Approval?

A pre-approval is a written statement from a lender that says, based on your income, credit, and debt, you qualify for a loan up to a certain dollar amount. It is not a guarantee, but it is a strong, informed estimate. Your lender reviews your actual documents, not just estimates, so the number is reliable.

A pre-qualification is different. With a pre-qualification, a lender gives you a rough estimate based on information you share verbally or online, without verifying documents. It is a starting point, but it does not carry the same weight as a full pre-approval.

If you want sellers in Gilmer to take your offer seriously, you want the pre-approval.

## What Do You Need to Get Pre-Approved?

The paperwork is not as intimidating as it sounds. Here is what most lenders ask for:

Pay stubs from the last 30 days. W-2s or 1099s from the last two years. Two months of bank statements. A government-issued ID. If you are self-employed or have other income sources, you may also need tax returns.

Your lender will also pull your credit report. They are looking at your credit score, your payment history, and how much debt you currently carry compared to your income. That last part, your debt to income ratio, is one of the biggest factors in how much you can borrow.

The whole process usually takes a day or two if you have your documents ready. Some lenders can issue a pre-approval letter within 24 hours.

## What Happens After Pre-Approval?

Once you have your pre-approval letter, you can start shopping with confidence. When you find a home and make an offer, you submit the letter along with it. If your offer is accepted, your lender moves into the full underwriting process.

During underwriting, the lender takes a closer look at all the details. They will order an appraisal to confirm the home's value, and they will do a final review of your finances. As long as nothing major changes in your situation between pre-approval and closing, the process moves forward.

A few things to avoid during this time: do not open new credit cards or take out new loans, do not make large unexplained deposits into your bank accounts, and do not change jobs if you can help it. These things can change your financial picture and delay or affect your approval.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Mortgage Pre-Approval in Gilmer TX

**How long does a pre-approval last?**
Most pre-approval letters are valid for 60 to 90 days. If your home search takes longer than that, your lender can usually refresh the approval with updated documents.

**Does getting pre-approved hurt my credit score?**
Yes, a little, but not much. A mortgage pre-approval involves a hard credit inquiry, which can lower your score by a few points. If you apply with multiple lenders within a short window, around 14 to 45 days, the credit bureaus typically count it as one inquiry. The impact fades quickly and is worth the benefit of knowing your real buying power.

**Do I have to use the lender I got pre-approved with?**
No. A pre-approval from one lender does not lock you in. You can shop around and choose whoever offers you the best rate and terms when you are ready to apply. Some buyers get pre-approved early just to know their number, then compare lenders more carefully before closing.

**What if my pre-approval amount is lower than I hoped?**
That is actually useful information. It means you can work on paying down debt, saving more, or adjusting your expectations before you are under contract. Your lender can show you exactly what would need to change to increase your buying power.

**Can I get pre-approved if I am self-employed in Gilmer?**
Yes. Self-employed buyers can absolutely get pre-approved. You will need to provide more documentation, usually two years of tax returns and potentially a profit and loss statement, but there are loan programs designed for people who work for themselves.
