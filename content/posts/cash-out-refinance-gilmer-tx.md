## What Is a Cash Out Refinance?

A regular refinance swaps your old mortgage for a new one at a better rate or term. A cash out refinance does the same thing, but you borrow more than you currently owe. The extra amount comes to you at closing as a lump sum of cash.

Here is a simple example. Say your home is worth $200,000 and you still owe $120,000. You have $80,000 in equity. With a cash out refinance, you might take out a new loan for $160,000. You pay off the $120,000 you owed, and $40,000 lands in your pocket.

Your new loan will have a new interest rate and a new monthly payment. The goal is to find a good rate so that payment stays comfortable for your budget.

## Do You Qualify for a Cash Out Refinance?

Lenders look at a few key things when you apply.

**Credit score.** Most programs want a score of at least 620. A higher score often means a better interest rate for you.

**Equity.** You generally need to keep at least 20 percent equity in your home after the cash out. That protects the lender and keeps your loan within safe limits.

**Income and debt.** Lenders want to see that your monthly income comfortably covers your new payment plus all your other bills. That ratio of debt to income is a big factor in whether you get approved.

**Property condition.** If your home needs major repairs, that can affect the appraisal and loan approval. The lender wants to know the home is in decent shape.

## FAQ: Cash Out Refinance in Gilmer TX

**Can I do a cash out refinance if I bought my home recently?**
Most lenders require you to have owned your home for at least six months before doing a cash out refinance. Some programs ask for 12 months. It depends on the loan type and the lender.

**Will a cash out refinance hurt my credit?**
Applying for any mortgage creates a small, temporary dip in your score. Over time, making payments on time will help your credit recover and often improve.

**How much cash can I actually get out?**
That depends on your home value and what you still owe. Most programs cap the new loan at 80 percent of the appraised value. A quick conversation with a broker can give you a real number based on your specific home.

**Is the cash from a cash out refinance taxable?**
The money you receive is not considered income, so it is generally not taxed. Tax rules can be complex though. Talk to a tax professional about your specific situation before making any decisions.

**What is the difference between a cash out refinance and a home equity loan?**
A home equity loan is a second loan you take on top of your existing mortgage. A cash out refinance replaces your first mortgage entirely with a new one. Which option works better depends on your current rate, how much cash you need, and your overall goals.
