## What Is a USDA Loan and How Does It Work?

A USDA loan is a government backed mortgage offered through the United States Department of Agriculture. Despite the name, you don't need to farm anything or live on a ranch. The program was created to help moderate income buyers purchase homes in rural and suburban areas across the country.

The biggest benefit is zero down payment. You can buy a home without saving up thousands of dollars first. USDA loans also come with competitive interest rates and lower mortgage insurance costs compared to FHA loans. That combination makes them one of the most affordable paths to homeownership for people who meet the requirements.

## USDA Loans in Fort Collins CO: The Honest Truth About Location

Here is something important to understand upfront. Fort Collins itself, especially areas near Colorado State University and downtown, does not qualify for USDA financing. The program is designed for rural and suburban areas, and the densely populated parts of the city fall outside those boundaries.

But here is the good news. Many areas just outside Fort Collins city limits do qualify. Rural pockets of Larimer County, smaller communities in the surrounding region, and homes in less dense corridors on the outskirts of the Front Range may be USDA eligible. You can check any specific property address on the USDA eligibility map at the USDA website.

If you are open to living a bit outside the city core, USDA can save you a significant amount of money at closing. For many buyers in Northern Colorado, that flexibility makes a real difference.

## Income Limits for USDA Loans Near Fort Collins

USDA loans are designed for moderate income households, which means there are income caps you need to stay under. These limits are based on household size and the specific location of the home.

For areas around Fort Collins and Larimer County, the income limits are adjusted to reflect the higher cost of living in Northern Colorado. A family of four can often earn well into six figures and still qualify, depending on the year and location.

One thing that surprises many buyers is how USDA calculates income. USDA looks at your total household income, not just the income of the people applying for the loan. If someone lives in your home and earns money, that income counts toward the limit even if they are not on the mortgage. A local mortgage broker will walk through this calculation with you before you commit to anything.

## What Properties Qualify for USDA Financing?

The property itself also has to meet USDA requirements. Here is what you need to know.

The home must be your primary residence. You cannot use a USDA loan for a vacation property or a rental. The property also needs to be in good condition and meet basic livability standards set by USDA appraisers.

Single family homes are the most common type of USDA purchase. Some condominiums and manufactured homes may qualify depending on the project and whether it meets agency guidelines. Your mortgage broker can confirm eligibility for a specific property before you put in an offer, which saves you from falling in love with a home that doesn't work out.

## How USDA Compares to FHA and Conventional Loans in the Fort Collins Market

If you are weighing your options, here is a plain side by side look at the most common low and no down payment programs.

FHA loans require at least 3.5% down but have far fewer location restrictions. You can use an FHA loan inside Fort Collins city limits, which makes it a better fit for buyers who want to live close to CSU or the Old Town area.

USDA loans require zero down but are limited to eligible rural and suburban areas. If you are comfortable living outside the Fort Collins core and you meet the income limits, USDA puts the most money back in your pocket at closing.

Conventional loans often require anywhere from 3% to 20% down depending on your credit score and situation. They come with no location restrictions and no income limits, but you will generally need stronger credit to get the most favorable terms.

The right loan depends on your specific income, credit score, and where you want to live. A local mortgage broker who knows Larimer County can help you compare programs side by side so you pick the one that actually fits your life.

## FAQ: USDA Loans in Fort Collins CO

**Can I use a USDA loan to buy a home inside Fort Collins?**
Most of Fort Collins city does not qualify because the population density is too high for USDA guidelines. Properties on the outskirts of the city or in rural Larimer County may qualify. Check specific addresses on the USDA eligibility map before assuming a property does or doesn't work.

**What credit score do I need for a USDA loan?**
Most lenders look for a credit score of at least 640. Some programs can work with scores below that, but lower scores typically require more documentation and a longer review process.

**How long does it take to close a USDA loan?**
USDA loans involve a government review step that can add one to two weeks compared to conventional loans. Plan ahead and start the process early, especially if you have a specific closing deadline.

**Do I need to be a first time homebuyer to use a USDA loan?**
No. USDA loans are open to repeat buyers as long as you meet the income and location requirements and are purchasing your primary residence.

**What fees come with a USDA loan?**
USDA loans include a one time upfront funding fee of 1% of the loan amount, which can be rolled into your loan. There is also an annual fee of 0.35% of the remaining loan balance. Both of these are typically lower than FHA mortgage insurance costs.
