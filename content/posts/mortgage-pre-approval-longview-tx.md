## What Is a Mortgage Pre-Approval?

A mortgage pre-approval is a written statement from a lender that tells you how much you qualify to borrow. To issue it, your lender looks at your credit score, your income, your monthly debts, and your assets. Then they give you a firm number.

This is different from a pre-qualification, which is just a rough estimate based on information you share without any verification. Pre-qualification is a fine starting point, but sellers in Longview know the difference. When two buyers make offers and one has a verified pre-approval letter and one does not, sellers almost always go with the pre-approved buyer.

## How the Pre-Approval Process Works

Getting a mortgage pre-approval in Longview TX takes a few steps, and most of them are straightforward.

First, you complete a loan application. This covers your employment, income, monthly expenses, and the type of home you want to buy. Most applications take less than 30 minutes to fill out.

Second, your lender pulls your credit. This is called a hard inquiry, and it may lower your credit score by a few points temporarily. The effect is small and short-lived.

Third, you gather and upload your documents. Your lender reviews them, and then issues your pre-approval letter. From application to letter, the process usually takes one to three business days.

Once you have that letter, you are ready to shop with confidence.

## Documents You Need to Get Pre-Approved

Here is what most lenders ask for:

Your two most recent pay stubs. Your W-2 forms from the last two years. Federal tax returns if you are self-employed or have income from multiple sources. Two months of bank statements. A government-issued photo ID.

If you earn income from a rental property, a side business, or another source, bring documentation for that too. The more complete your picture, the smoother the process goes. Having everything ready upfront saves back-and-forth time and gets your letter to you faster.

## How Long Does a Pre-Approval Last?

Most pre-approval letters are good for 60 to 90 days. If your home search runs longer than that, your lender can usually refresh the letter with updated pay stubs and bank statements and a new credit pull.

The best time to get pre-approved is when you are actively ready to look at homes. If you get pre-approved and then wait several months before touring, you will probably need to renew your letter before making an offer. Plan the timing so your letter is fresh when you need it most.

## What to Avoid After You Get Pre-Approved

Once your lender issues your pre-approval, keep your finances steady. Avoid opening new credit cards or taking on new loans. Do not make any large purchases before closing. Do not change jobs if you can help it, because a change in employment can raise questions during underwriting.

Your lender will do a final review of your finances before closing to make sure nothing has changed. If your financial picture looks the same as it did during pre-approval, you are in good shape.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Mortgage Pre-Approval in Longview TX

**Does a mortgage pre-approval hurt my credit score?**

Yes, a little. Getting pre-approved involves a hard credit pull, which can lower your score by a few points. But the impact is small and temporary. If you apply with a few different lenders within a short window, usually 14 to 45 days, credit bureaus typically count it as one inquiry, not several.

**Can I get pre-approved if I am self-employed?**

Yes. Self-employed buyers can absolutely get pre-approved. You will typically need two years of tax returns and possibly a profit and loss statement. Some lenders also offer bank statement loans, where your deposits are used to verify income rather than tax forms. A mortgage broker can help match you with the right program.

**What if my pre-approval amount is lower than I expected?**

That is actually useful to know early. Your lender can show you exactly what it would take to increase your buying power, whether that means paying down some debt or saving a larger down payment. It is much better to find this out before you fall in love with a home that does not fit your budget.

**How much do I need for a down payment?**

It depends on the loan type. FHA loans allow as little as 3.5 percent down. Conventional loans can start as low as 3 percent in some cases. VA loans require no down payment at all for qualifying veterans and service members. Your lender will walk you through what makes sense for your situation and goals.

**Can I change lenders after I get pre-approved?**

Yes. A pre-approval from one lender does not lock you into using them. You are free to compare rates and terms and choose whoever offers you the best deal when you are ready to move forward.
