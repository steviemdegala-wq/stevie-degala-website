## What Makes a Manufactured Home Loan Different

A manufactured home is built entirely in a factory and then transported to your land. That process is different from a traditional build, so lenders pay attention to a few specific things.

The two most important questions lenders ask are: Is the home on a permanent foundation? And is it titled as real property rather than personal property?

If your manufactured home sits on land you own and has a permanent foundation, you can often qualify for FHA, VA, USDA, or conventional financing. If the home is on leased land or titled like a vehicle, your options get narrower. But options still exist, and the right lender will help you figure out which path fits your situation.

## Manufactured Home Loans in Longview TX: Your Main Options

**FHA Loans**
FHA loans are one of the most popular choices for buyers in Longview and Gregg County. You need at least a 580 credit score for a 3.5% down payment. The home must sit on a permanent foundation and meet HUD standards. FHA is a great fit if you have limited savings or are buying your first home.

**VA Loans**
Longview has a strong veteran community, and VA loans are built for it. If you served in the military or are on active duty, a VA loan often requires no down payment at all. The home must meet VA property standards and be on a permanent foundation. This is one of the strongest programs available, and it is worth exploring if you qualify.

**USDA Loans**
Some areas in and around Longview qualify for USDA rural housing loans, which allow qualified buyers to purchase with no down payment. The home must be classified as real property on a permanent foundation, and income limits apply. It helps to run the numbers early to see if your household qualifies.

**Conventional Loans**
Conventional loans for manufactured homes are available for buyers with stronger credit and larger down payments. The requirements are stricter than government backed programs, but the loan terms can be competitive. The home must be on a permanent foundation and titled as real estate.

## The Permanent Foundation Requirement

The permanent foundation requirement comes up in almost every manufactured home transaction, so it is worth understanding from the start. Most loan programs need the home attached to a concrete perimeter, slab, or other fixed system that connects it permanently to the ground. Homes sitting only on piers or blocks often do not qualify for the better programs.

If the home you are considering does not have a permanent foundation, it may be possible to have one installed before closing. That adds to your upfront cost, but it can unlock much better loan programs and lower interest rates. A good lender will help you think through whether that tradeoff makes sense in your case.

## What Lenders Look At When You Apply

When you apply for a manufactured home loan, lenders look at the same core things they look at for any mortgage, plus a few extras specific to manufactured homes.

Your credit score matters. A score above 620 opens up more options, though FHA works with scores as low as 580. Your income and employment history are reviewed closely, especially how long you have been with your employer. Lenders also look at the loan-to-value ratio, which is how much you are borrowing compared to the home's appraised value.

The home itself goes through an appraisal. The appraiser looks at the condition, the foundation, and what comparable manufactured homes nearby have sold for. Finding good comparables in East Texas can sometimes take a little extra work. A lender who knows the Longview and Gregg County market will make that process go more smoothly.

## FAQ

**Can I get a manufactured home loan with a lower credit score?**
Yes. FHA loans allow scores as low as 580 with a 3.5% down payment. Some programs work with scores below 580, though the terms will not be as favorable. The best step is to have a real conversation with a local broker so you understand exactly where you stand.

**Does the home need to be on land that I own?**
For FHA, VA, and USDA programs, yes. The home should be on your own land with a permanent foundation. There are chattel loans available for homes on leased land, but those come with higher rates and shorter repayment terms.

**What is the difference between a manufactured home and a mobile home?**
Manufactured homes built after June 15, 1976, are built under federal HUD safety codes and are typically eligible for mortgage financing. Older homes built before that date are often called mobile homes and face more restrictions. If you are looking at an older home, ask your lender about the age and title before you get too far into the process.

**How long does it take to close on a manufactured home?**
Usually 30 to 60 days. Rural and semi-rural appraisals in Gregg County can take a little extra time, so getting pre-approved and gathering your documents early helps everything move faster.

**Can Eastman Chemical employees get special loan programs?**
Not specifically tied to your employer, but stable W-2 employment like a job at Eastman Chemical is viewed very favorably by lenders. It makes qualifying easier and can help you get better terms. If you have worked there for at least two years, that is a strong foundation for a mortgage application.
