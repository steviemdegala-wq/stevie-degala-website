## Why Self Employed Borrowers Face Extra Hurdles

Most mortgage programs are built around the idea that you get a paycheck from an employer every two weeks. That paycheck is easy to verify and easy to explain to an underwriter. When you work for yourself, your income looks different on paper.

You might write off business expenses that lower your taxable income. That is smart tax planning. But it can also make your income look lower than it really is to a lender who only looks at your tax returns. A good mortgage broker knows how to read your returns the right way and find the income that actually counts toward your loan.

Some lenders will also look at your bank statements instead of your tax returns. This can work very well for self employed borrowers whose deposits are strong even when their taxable income looks modest.

## Loan Options for Self Employed Buyers in Longview TX

You have several real paths to homeownership even without a W2.

**Bank Statement Loans.** Instead of tax returns, the lender looks at 12 or 24 months of business or personal bank statements to calculate your average monthly income. This works well if your deposits are consistent and your business is healthy. Lenders typically use a percentage of your gross deposits to arrive at your qualifying income.

**1099 Loans.** If you work as an independent contractor and receive 1099 forms, some programs will use your 1099s to verify income. This can be simpler than going through full tax return analysis.

**Profit and Loss Statement Loans.** Some lenders will accept a CPA prepared profit and loss statement covering the most recent one or two years. Combined with bank statements, this can give underwriters a clear picture of your business income.

**Conventional Loans with Full Documentation.** If your tax returns show enough income after deductions, you may still qualify for a conventional loan. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac allow self employment income, but they require two years of returns and may adjust how they calculate your income based on depreciation, depletion, and business use of assets.

## What Lenders Look for When You Are Self Employed

Every lender who works with self employed borrowers will want to see a few things.

First, they want to see at least two years in business. Some exceptions exist for people who recently transitioned from a related field of employment, but generally you need to show that your business has been running for a couple of years.

Second, they want to see that your income is stable or growing. A business that earned more this year than last year tells a good story. A business that showed a big drop raises questions.

Third, they want to see that you have good credit. Self employed borrowers do not get any extra room on credit score requirements just because their income is harder to document. Keep your credit strong and pay your debts on time.

Fourth, they will look at your debt to income ratio. That is the comparison between what you owe each month and what you earn. Even with alternative income documentation, this ratio matters.

## How to Prepare Your Application

A little preparation goes a long way when you are self employed and shopping for a mortgage.

Gather your last two years of personal and business tax returns. Even if you plan to use a bank statement loan, having returns available is useful.

Compile 12 to 24 months of bank statements for all accounts your lender might want to review. Business and personal accounts both matter depending on the program.

Get your credit in good shape before you apply. Pay down revolving balances if you can, and avoid taking on new debt in the months before your purchase.

Talk to a mortgage broker before you talk to a bank. A broker has access to multiple lenders and programs. A bank can only offer what they have in house. For self employed borrowers, having options is everything.

## FAQ

**Can I get a mortgage in Longview TX if my tax returns show low income?**
Yes. Bank statement loan programs and other alternative documentation options look at your actual cash flow rather than your taxable income. These programs exist specifically for borrowers whose returns do not tell the full story.

**How long do I need to be self employed to get a mortgage?**
Most programs want to see at least two years of self employment history. Some programs will consider one year if you recently moved from a related W2 job into a similar field.

**Do I need a bigger down payment if I am self employed?**
Not necessarily. Some bank statement programs do require 10 to 20 percent down, but others allow less depending on your credit and loan amount. Conventional programs follow standard down payment guidelines even for self employed borrowers who qualify.

**Will I pay a higher interest rate?**
Alternative documentation loans like bank statement programs sometimes carry slightly higher rates than fully documented conventional loans. The difference varies by lender and your overall profile. A broker can help you compare options and find the best fit.
