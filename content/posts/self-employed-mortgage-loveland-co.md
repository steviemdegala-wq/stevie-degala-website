Getting a self-employed mortgage in Loveland CO is well within reach. Loveland has a strong community of entrepreneurs, artists, contractors, and small business owners — and the mortgage industry has programs specifically designed for borrowers who do not earn a traditional W-2 income.

The process is a bit different than a standard application, but the right loan structure makes approval realistic even when your tax return does not reflect your full earning picture.

## What Lenders Actually Want to See

The challenge with self-employment is income verification. Lenders need to confirm that your income is real, stable, and likely to continue. When you receive a W-2, that is easy. When you run a business or work independently, lenders need more documentation to get comfortable.

Most conventional lenders will ask for two years of federal tax returns, a year-to-date profit and loss statement, and sometimes business bank statements. They calculate your qualifying income using a specific formula that accounts for your business structure. The problem is that this formula often results in a lower number than what you actually take home, because business deductions reduce the income they count.

## Bank Statement Loans for Self-Employed Loveland Buyers

A bank statement loan solves the tax return problem directly. Instead of using your taxable income, the lender averages your actual bank deposits over 12 to 24 months. If your cash flow tells a better story than your tax return, this is often the most powerful tool available to you.

This program works especially well for business owners in Loveland who write off significant expenses — home offices, vehicles, equipment, travel — and end up with a taxable income that does not reflect their real financial position. You will generally need a credit score of 620 or higher and a down payment of 10 to 20 percent. Rates run slightly above conventional programs, but the qualifying flexibility makes it worth it for many borrowers.

## Conventional Loans When Your Returns Support It

If your tax returns do show enough qualifying income, a conventional loan gives you access to competitive rates and a wide range of programs. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac both allow self-employed borrowers with at least two years of self-employment history in the same field to qualify using their tax return income.

Loveland falls within standard conforming loan limits in Larimer County, so you have the full range of conventional programs available. Down payments start as low as three to five percent on some programs, and a credit score of 620 or higher is typically required.

## Getting Your Documents Together

For a bank statement loan: 12 to 24 months of personal or business bank statements, a profit and loss statement from a CPA, and documentation of self-employment such as a business license or CPA letter.

For a conventional loan: two years of personal tax returns, two years of business tax returns if applicable, a year-to-date profit and loss statement, and recent bank statements.

Pulling these together before your first conversation with a lender saves time and prevents delays once your application is in process.

## FAQ: Self-Employed Mortgage in Loveland CO

**Do I need two full years of self-employment history?**

For conventional and FHA loans, typically yes. Some bank statement programs accept one year if you have at least two years of prior experience in the same industry. Your broker will review your specific situation.

**Will my business write-offs hurt my mortgage application?**

On a conventional loan, yes — because lenders use taxable income. On a bank statement loan, no — deposits are used instead. Choosing the right program based on your tax situation is one of the most important decisions in this process.

**What credit score do I need?**

Conventional and bank statement loans generally require 620 or higher. FHA loans can go as low as 580. A stronger score always improves the rates available to you.

**My income varies month to month. Is that a problem?**

Variable income is normal with self-employment and lenders account for it. They will average your income over 12 or 24 months to arrive at a consistent qualifying number. An overall upward trend in deposits is viewed positively.

**Is Loveland a good market to buy in if I am self-employed?**

Yes. Home prices here are more accessible than Fort Collins while still offering strong community amenities and long-term value. Getting the right loan structure in place gives self-employed buyers a real path to ownership here.
