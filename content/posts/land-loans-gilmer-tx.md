## What Is a Land Loan?

A land loan is a loan that lets you borrow money to buy a piece of property with no home on it yet. Because there is no house for the lender to use as collateral, land loans are seen as a bit riskier than regular mortgages. That usually means a larger down payment and a slightly higher interest rate. But if owning land in Gilmer is your goal, a land loan is the right tool for the job.

## Raw Land vs. Improved Land: What You Need to Know

Not all land is treated the same by lenders. Understanding the difference can help you plan your budget before you fall in love with a listing.

Raw land is fully undeveloped. No utilities, no road access, nothing built on the property at all. Lenders see raw land as the riskiest type, so you may need a down payment of 30 to 50 percent.

Improved land already has utilities run to the property, a road or driveway in place, and sometimes a well and septic system ready to go. Because improved land is closer to being ready for use, lenders are more comfortable with it. Down payments can be lower, sometimes around 20 to 30 percent.

In Upshur County you will find both. Plenty of people in the Gilmer area purchase raw timber land or open pasture, while others buy parcels that are already set up for a home site. Knowing which type you are looking at will save you from surprises at the closing table.

## Land Loans in Gilmer TX: What Lenders Look At

When you apply for land loans in Gilmer TX, lenders review several things beyond just your credit score.

Your credit score matters. A score of 660 or above gives you the most options. Some lenders will work with lower scores but expect stricter terms in return.

Your down payment amount matters too. Plan on putting down at least 20 percent for improved land and potentially more if the property is remote or undeveloped.

Your plans for the land also play a role. Lenders want to know if you plan to build, farm, or hold the property as an investment. Your answer can affect which loan programs you qualify for.

The land itself gets reviewed closely. Lenders look at the size, location, road access, and utility availability. Acreage near Gilmer with easy road access and utilities already in place is generally much easier to finance than a remote wooded tract.

Getting this information together before your first conversation with a lender makes the whole process go a lot smoother.

## USDA and Farm Credit Options for East Texas Buyers

If you are buying rural acreage in Gilmer or anywhere in the Upshur County area, a couple of programs are worth knowing about.

USDA farm loans through the Farm Service Agency are designed for buyers who want to purchase agricultural land in rural areas. This is a separate program from the USDA home loan, which covers homes and not bare land. If you plan to farm or ranch the property, this is worth asking about.

Farm Credit lenders specialize in rural and agricultural property across East Texas. They understand local land values and are comfortable lending on larger acreage tracts that big national banks sometimes turn down. Working with a lender who knows this market makes a real difference.

## Building on Your Land Later: How It Works

Many buyers in Gilmer purchase land first and plan to build later. That is a perfectly sensible approach, but there are a few things to keep in mind.

Most land loans are shorter in term than a regular 30 year mortgage. Terms of five to fifteen years are common, and some loans include a balloon payment at the end, meaning the remaining balance comes due all at once.

When you are ready to build, you can apply for a construction loan. One popular option is a construction to permanent loan, which starts as financing for the build and then rolls into a regular mortgage once the home is complete. This keeps you from having to apply for two separate loans.

One other advantage: if you already own your land free and clear, the equity in that land can count toward your down payment when you apply for a construction loan. Buyers who plan ahead this way often end up with better terms and a smoother process.

## FAQ: Land Loans in Gilmer TX

**Can I get a land loan with no money down?**

Most land loans require a down payment. Zero down options are not common for bare land. Plan on at least 20 percent for improved land and potentially more for raw or remote acreage.

**How long does a land loan last?**

Terms are usually shorter than a standard mortgage. Five to fifteen years is common, though some lenders offer up to 20 years. Ask about balloon payment terms so there are no surprises later.

**Can I use a land loan and then build a house on the property?**

Yes. Many buyers do exactly that. You get the land loan first, then a construction loan when you are ready to build. Some lenders offer a combined program that covers both steps in one package.

**Is land near Gilmer hard to finance?**

It depends on the property. Improved parcels with road access and utilities near Gilmer are generally straightforward to finance. Remote raw land without utilities can be trickier. A lender familiar with Upshur County property will give you the most accurate picture.

**What if there is an old structure or mobile home on the land?**

Tell your lender upfront. An existing structure, even an old barn or mobile home, can affect how the loan is classified and which programs you qualify for. Transparency early on prevents problems later.
