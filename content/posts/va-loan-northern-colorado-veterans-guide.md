If you have served in the U.S. military and you are buying a home in Northern Colorado, you likely have access to the most powerful mortgage benefit available to any borrower. Zero down payment. No private mortgage insurance. Rates that compete with anything in the market.

The problem is that too many veterans either do not know the full extent of what they have earned, or they walk into a lender that talks them out of it in favor of a conventional product. This post covers the VA loan in full — what it is, how it works in the Fort Collins and Northern Colorado market, who qualifies, and what to watch out for.

## What a VA Loan Is

A VA loan is a mortgage backed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. The VA does not lend you money directly. Instead, they guarantee a portion of the loan, which reduces the lender's risk. Because of that guarantee, lenders can offer terms they would never extend to a conventional borrower at the same down payment level — including zero down with no mortgage insurance.

The program has been running since 1944. It has helped more than 28 million veterans and service members buy homes. And it remains, by most measures, the most favorable loan structure available in the residential mortgage market.

## The Core Benefits

**Zero down payment.** You do not need to save up 3%, 5%, or 20% before you can buy. If you qualify for a VA loan, you can purchase a home in Fort Collins, Loveland, Windsor, or anywhere else in Northern Colorado with nothing down. That is not a promotional offer — it is the baseline.

**No private mortgage insurance.** PMI is a monthly fee that conventional borrowers pay when they put less than 20% down. It protects the lender, not you, and it adds real cost — often $150 to $350 per month depending on loan size and credit profile. VA loans have no PMI requirement, period.

**Competitive interest rates.** Because the VA guarantees part of the loan, lenders take on less risk. That reduced risk typically translates to lower rates than what the same borrower would receive on a conventional loan. VA rates are not always the lowest available, but they are consistently among the most competitive.

**Flexible credit guidelines.** The VA does not set a minimum credit score. Individual lenders do, but VA guidelines are generally more forgiving than conventional loan standards, particularly for borrowers who have had challenges in the past.

**No prepayment penalty.** You can pay off your VA loan early, make extra payments, or refinance without penalty.

## Who Is Eligible

Eligibility for a VA loan is based on your service history. You generally qualify if you fall into one of these groups:

**Veterans** who were discharged under conditions other than dishonorable after meeting minimum service requirements. The specific length of service required varies depending on when you served and the nature of your service.

**Active duty service members** who have served a minimum number of continuous days on active duty. Generally, 90 consecutive days during wartime or 181 days during peacetime.

**National Guard and Reserve members** with at least six years of service, or those who were called to active duty under Title 10 or Title 32 orders and served the required number of days.

**Surviving spouses** of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability, who have not remarried — or who remarried after age 57.

If you are not sure whether you qualify, that is exactly what a Certificate of Eligibility is for. I pull COEs directly as part of the loan process. If the entitlement is there, we will find it quickly.

## The Certificate of Eligibility

The Certificate of Eligibility — the COE — is the document that confirms to your lender that you meet the VA's service requirements. You need one to use your VA loan benefit.

Getting it is not complicated. I can pull your COE through VA systems directly in most cases, within minutes. If your records require additional documentation, I will walk you through exactly what to gather. You do not need to navigate the VA website on your own or wait for paperwork to arrive in the mail before we can move forward.

## The VA Funding Fee

The one cost unique to VA loans that surprises some buyers is the funding fee. This is a one-time fee paid at closing — or rolled into the loan balance — that helps sustain the VA loan program for future veterans.

The amount depends on a few factors:

- Whether this is your first VA loan or a subsequent use
- How much you put down, if anything
- Your branch of service

For a first-time VA loan buyer with no down payment, the funding fee is currently 2.15% of the loan amount. On a $500,000 home, that is $10,750. It can be rolled into the loan so you do not need to bring it to closing.

**The funding fee is waived entirely for veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or greater.** Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability are also exempt. This is one of the most significant and underutilized aspects of the program — I confirm your status early so we know exactly what your closing costs look like before you go under contract.

## VA Loan Programs Available to You

### VA Purchase Loan

The standard program — buy a home with zero down, no PMI, and a government-backed rate. Works for single-family homes, townhomes, condos (on approved lists), and new construction with some additional steps.

### VA Cash-Out Refinance

If you already own a home and have built equity, a VA cash-out refinance lets you access that equity — up to 100% of your home's appraised value in many cases. This can be used to pay off high-interest debt, fund a home improvement, or cover any other financial goal. It is also the mechanism that allows non-VA loan holders to convert their existing mortgage into a VA loan and access VA benefits going forward.

### VA IRRRL — Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan

If you already have a VA loan and rates have dropped since you closed, the IRRRL — commonly called the VA streamline refinance — is one of the fastest refinance options in the mortgage market. In most cases, it requires no appraisal, no income verification, and minimal documentation. The goal is simple: lower your rate and your payment with as little friction as possible.

## What the VA Appraisal Process Looks Like

VA loans require a VA-ordered appraisal, separate from any inspection you choose to get. The appraiser is assigned by the VA, not selected by the lender. The appraisal determines the market value of the home and checks for Minimum Property Requirements — the VA's baseline standards for safety, habitability, and structural soundness.

For most homes in good condition, the MPR review is not an obstacle. Older homes or properties with visible deferred maintenance can occasionally require repairs before closing. I flag potential MPR issues early in the process so there are no surprises once you are under contract.

## When a Conventional Loan Might Be Worth Considering

VA loans are not automatically the right choice in every scenario. A few situations where the comparison is worth running:

**You have 20% down saved.** If you are putting 20% down, you avoid PMI on a conventional loan — and conventional rates at that tier are highly competitive. The funding fee on a VA loan adds cost that you would not incur on the conventional side. Run both numbers.

**The property is a condo not on the VA approved list.** VA loans require that condos be on the VA's approved condominium project list. If you find a condo you love that is not on the list, the approval process can take time. A conventional loan has no equivalent restriction.

**You are purchasing an investment property.** VA loans are limited to primary residences. For rental properties or investment acquisitions, a DSCR loan or conventional investor product is the right structure.

Outside of these scenarios, VA is almost always worth at least running the comparison.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use my VA benefit more than once?**
Yes. VA loan benefits can be used multiple times throughout your life. As long as you have remaining entitlement — or you sell the previous home and restore your entitlement — you can use a VA loan again.

**Is there a loan limit on VA loans in Northern Colorado?**
For veterans with full entitlement, there is no official cap on the loan amount, as long as your income supports the payment and the lender approves the file. Loan limits only apply if you have remaining entitlement from a prior VA loan that was not paid off.

**Can I use a VA loan for new construction in Timnath or Windsor?**
Yes, with some additional steps. New construction VA loans require a VA-approved appraisal before or during the build and typically a one-year builder warranty. It is not uncommon — I work through new construction VA transactions regularly in Northern Colorado's growing communities.

**My credit took a hit during my service. Does that disqualify me?**
Not necessarily. VA guidelines are more flexible than conventional, and I work with lenders who understand the financial circumstances that can come with service-connected challenges. The best way to know where you stand is to pull your credit and review it together before assuming anything.

**Does my surviving spouse qualify?**
The unmarried surviving spouse of a veteran who died in service or from a service-connected disability is generally eligible for the VA loan benefit. If you remarried after age 57, you may still qualify. I confirm eligibility as the first step, no assumptions made.

**I have a disability rating. Does that change anything?**
A service-connected disability rating of 10% or greater waives the VA funding fee entirely. A rating of 50% or higher may also qualify you for a property tax exemption in Colorado, which reduces your monthly carrying cost further. Both are worth confirming early.
