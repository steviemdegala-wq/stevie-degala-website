## What Is a Jumbo Loan?

A jumbo loan is simply a home loan that is too large to be bought by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. Those are the two large government backed companies that purchase most mortgages after closing. There is a dollar amount called the conforming loan limit. Any loan below that number is a conventional loan. Any loan above it is a jumbo loan.

Because jumbo loans come from private lenders rather than being backed by the government, lenders take on more risk. That means they look more carefully at your credit history, your income, and how much you have in savings. Meeting the qualifications takes a little more preparation, but it is very doable.

## What Lenders Look For With Jumbo Loans

Because these loans are not backed by Fannie or Freddie, lenders want to feel confident about the borrower. Here is what they typically look at.

**Credit score.** Most jumbo lenders want to see a score of at least 700, and many prefer 720 or higher. The stronger your score, the more programs and better rates you will likely qualify for.

**Debt to income ratio.** This is the percentage of your monthly income that goes toward debt payments. Lenders generally want this below 43 percent, though some programs offer more flexibility for borrowers with strong overall profiles.

**Cash reserves.** Many jumbo lenders want to see that you have several months of mortgage payments sitting in the bank after you close. This shows them you could handle a rough patch without missing a payment.

**Income documentation.** You will need to document your income carefully. Pay stubs, tax returns, and W2s are all common requirements. If you work for yourself, plan on providing two years of tax returns and a current profit and loss statement.

**Down payment.** Many jumbo programs ask for 10 to 20 percent down. Some go as low as 5 percent for borrowers with very strong profiles. It is worth asking what options are available for your exact situation.

## Jumbo Loan Rates Compared to Conventional Loans

Jumbo loan rates are set by the private market rather than government guidelines. In many market conditions, jumbo rates land close to conventional rates. They can be slightly higher or, in some environments, even lower depending on what is happening in the broader economy.

The best way to know your actual rate is to get prequalified. That gives you a real number tied to your credit profile and loan size, not a general estimate from a website. A local mortgage broker can shop multiple jumbo lenders at once to find you the most competitive option.

## Jumbo Loans vs. Other Approaches in Windsor

Before you lock in on a jumbo loan, it is worth knowing about a couple of alternatives.

A piggyback loan splits your financing into two parts: a first mortgage up to the conforming limit and a second loan for the rest. This can sometimes produce a better overall rate or structure, though it adds some complexity to the process.

Some buyers choose to make a larger down payment to bring the loan amount below the conforming limit. If you have the savings, this can simplify your financing and sometimes save money over the long run.

Running these scenarios side by side with a mortgage broker who knows the Northern Colorado market is the best way to figure out which approach fits your goals.

## FAQ: Jumbo Loans in Windsor, CO

**What is the conforming loan limit in Weld County?**

Loan limits are set by federal guidelines and can change each year. The best move is to confirm the current limit with your lender before you start shopping. What counts as a jumbo loan today may be different from last year.

**Can I qualify for a jumbo loan if I am self employed?**

Yes. Borrowers who work for themselves can absolutely qualify. You will typically need two years of tax returns and a current profit and loss statement. Lenders want to see consistent income that is clearly documented.

**Do jumbo loans require private mortgage insurance?**

Most jumbo loans do not require mortgage insurance, even with less than 20 percent down. Each lender has different rules, so confirm when you compare programs.

**How long does it take to close a jumbo loan?**

Plan for 30 to 45 days in most cases. Jumbo loans require a bit more documentation review than conventional loans, so giving yourself a little extra time is smart, especially in a competitive market.
