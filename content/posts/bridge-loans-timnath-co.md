## What Is a Bridge Loan?

A bridge loan is a short term loan, usually six to twelve months, that uses the equity in your current home to help you buy a new one. Think of it as a temporary financial connection between your old home and your new one. You borrow against what you already own, buy the next home, and then pay the loan off when your current home sells.

It is not a permanent mortgage. It is a short term tool to close a timing gap.

## How Bridge Loans Work in Timnath CO

Here is how the process usually looks. You have a home in Timnath or the surrounding Larimer County area, and you have built up equity over the years. You find a new home you want to buy, but you have not sold your current place yet.

A bridge loan lets you tap into that equity now. Your lender structures the loan so you can make an offer on the new home without a home sale contingency. That is a big deal in Northern Colorado, where sellers often receive multiple offers and may not want to wait for a buyer to sell first.

Once your current home sells, you use those proceeds to pay off the bridge loan. The whole thing can wrap up in just a few months.

## Who Is a Good Fit for a Bridge Loan?

Bridge loans are not for everyone, but they work really well if you fit any of these situations.

You have strong equity in your current home. Bridge loans typically require that you have at least 20 percent equity to work with.

You are confident your current home will sell in a reasonable timeframe. Because you are carrying two properties temporarily, you want to be realistic about your local market.

You need to move quickly. If you found a home in Timnath that you love and competition is fierce, a bridge loan lets you act without delay.

You want to avoid moving twice. Some buyers sell first, move into temporary housing, and then search again. A bridge loan lets you go straight from one home to the next.

## Bridge Loan Costs and What to Expect

It is important to go in with clear expectations. Bridge loans tend to have higher interest rates than a standard 30 year mortgage, often somewhere in the range of one to two percentage points higher. Because the loan is short term, the extra interest cost is usually manageable when weighed against the benefit of acting quickly.

You may also have closing costs on both the bridge loan and your new mortgage. Your lender will walk you through all the numbers before you commit, so there are no surprises.

The good news is that many bridge loan programs let you make interest only payments during the bridge period. That keeps your monthly outlay lower while you wait for your current home to sell.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get a bridge loan if I am still paying my current mortgage?**
Yes. Most bridge loan programs account for your existing mortgage as part of the calculation. Your lender will look at your overall equity and your ability to manage both payments temporarily.

**How long does a bridge loan last?**
Most bridge loans are written for six to twelve months. If your home has not sold by the end of that period, some lenders will work with you on an extension, though it is best to plan for a sale within the original window.

**Is a bridge loan the same as a home equity loan?**
No. A home equity loan is a longer term product, often 10 to 20 years, and is usually taken out when you plan to stay in your current home. A bridge loan is designed specifically to handle the transition between two homes.

**Do I need good credit to qualify?**
Bridge loans generally require solid credit and substantial equity. If your score needs work, your lender may have alternative options that accomplish a similar goal.

**What happens if my home takes longer to sell than expected?**
This is the main risk with a bridge loan. If your home sits longer than expected, you are carrying costs on two properties for a longer stretch. Having a good local agent and pricing your home right from the start is the best way to manage this.
