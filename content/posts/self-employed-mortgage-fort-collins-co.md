## What Makes a Self Employed Mortgage Different in Fort Collins?

The biggest difference is how lenders verify your income. When you get a W2 from an employer, verification is simple. When you own a business or work as a contractor, lenders want to see more documentation. They want proof that your income is stable and likely to continue.

Most conventional lenders will ask for two years of federal tax returns, a current year profit and loss statement, and sometimes business bank statements. They use a formula to calculate your qualifying income. Business deductions are great for reducing your tax bill, but they can also reduce the income a lender counts toward your mortgage. That tension is worth understanding before you apply.

## Bank Statement Loans for Self Employed Fort Collins Borrowers

Here is where things get interesting. If your tax returns show a lot of deductions and do not reflect your real cash flow, a bank statement loan might be a much better fit. This type of loan skips the tax returns entirely and uses your personal or business bank deposits instead, usually 12 to 24 months of them.

If you run a profitable business in Fort Collins but write off a lot of expenses, a bank statement loan can be the difference between qualifying and not qualifying. It is one of the most useful tools for self employed borrowers right now, and more lenders offer it than you might expect.

## Conventional Self Employed Mortgages in Larimer County

If your tax returns do show enough qualifying income, a conventional loan might be your best path. Conventional loans follow guidelines from Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac and offer competitive rates along with a wide range of down payment options.

For a conventional loan as a self employed borrower in Larimer County, you will typically need two years of self employment in the same field. You will also need a debt to income ratio, which means your total monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income, that fits within program guidelines. A solid credit score helps a lot here. Down payments can go as low as three percent on some programs.

## What Lenders Want to See in a Fort Collins Self Employed Application

A few things make a real difference when you apply. Consistency matters most. Lenders want income that is stable or growing, not swinging from year to year. If your income went up from year one to year two on your tax returns, that is a great story for an underwriter to read.

Keeping your business and personal finances separate helps too. A dedicated business checking account makes it easier for a lender to trace your income. Mixing business and personal money in one account can raise questions you do not want to answer in the middle of underwriting.

Your credit matters just as much here as it does for any other borrower. Pay your bills on time, keep credit card balances low, and avoid opening new accounts in the months before you apply.

## FAQ

**Do I need two full years of self employment to get a mortgage in Fort Collins?**
Usually yes for conventional loans. Some programs allow one year if you had at least two years in the same field before going out on your own. Bank statement loans have their own rules, so your broker can tell you exactly where you stand.

**Can I use a bank statement loan if I also have some W2 income?**
Yes. If you have a mix of self employed and W2 income, a lender can sometimes combine both sources to build your qualifying income picture.

**What credit score do I need as a self employed borrower?**
620 is often the minimum for conventional programs, but a score above 700 gives you much better rate and term options. The stronger your credit, the more programs are available to you.

**How much do I need to put down on a self employed mortgage in Fort Collins?**
It depends on the loan type. Conventional loans can go as low as three to five percent. Bank statement loans often require ten to twenty percent depending on the lender and your overall profile.

**Can I get preapproved before my taxes for this year are filed?**
It depends on the time of year. Lenders may accept a letter from your CPA or use the prior year return in some cases. Your mortgage broker can tell you exactly what documentation is needed at the time you apply.
